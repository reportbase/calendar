One boost, sorted

One Lorentz boost of rapidity ζ, taken apart. Each thing it changes is sorted by what a reader needs to have it: an address (a direction or a ratio: no unit, no clock), a payload (what arrives along an address, weighted), an amount (in a clock's or a rod's unit), or a convention (a choice, here Reichenbach's ε for distant simultaneity). Every row is checked live against the textbook formula.

What falls out. The boost's own parameter is a reading: the radar ratio k = √(t₂/t₁) = e^ζ needs one clock but not its unit, and no convention. Directions move by one Möbius map, a slide of ζ on the level ln tan(θ/2). The payload's weights are not a second action: D² is the Jacobian of that address map (arrivals per solid angle), and energy and intensity are its powers ½ and 2. The amounts are the clock's: the proper time √(t₁t₂) of the far event. And γ and β, read off the pair, depend on ε: the textbook values come at ε = ½, the choice that treats the out and back legs alike. There γ is the arithmetic mean of the pair over its geometric mean, (k + 1/k)/2. Away from ½ the speed read off the same pair can pass 1 (ε = 0.2 at ζ = 2.5 reads β ≈ 2.4): the convention moves the amount, never the ratio k.

What is classical. Aberration as a Möbius map of the celestial sphere (Penrose 1959; Terrell 1959); the Doppler factor and Iν/ν³ (Rybicki and Lightman 1979, §4.9); Bondi's k-calculus (1964); the conventionality of simultaneity (Reichenbach 1928; Winnie 1970). The sorting is this project's; SR's formalism already writes the pieces apart.
