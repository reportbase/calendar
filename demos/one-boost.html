One boost, sorted

One Lorentz boost of rapidity ζ, taken apart. Each thing it changes is sorted by what a reader needs to have it: an address (a direction or a ratio: no unit, no clock), a payload (what arrives along an address, weighted), an amount (in a clock's or a rod's unit), or a convention (a choice, here Reichenbach's ε for distant simultaneity). Every row is checked live against the textbook formula.

What falls out. The boost's own parameter is a reading: the radar ratio k = √(t₂/t₁) = e^ζ needs one clock but not its unit, and no convention. Directions move by one Möbius map, a slide of ζ on the level ln tan(θ/2). The payload's weights are not a second action: D² is the Jacobian of that address map (arrivals per solid angle), and energy and intensity are its powers ½ and 2. The amounts are the clock's: the proper time √(t₁t₂) of the far event. And γ and β, read off the pair, depend on ε: the textbook values come at ε = ½, the choice that treats the out and back legs alike. There γ is the arithmetic mean of the pair over its geometric mean, (k + 1/k)/2. Away from ½ the speed read off the same pair can pass 1 (ε = 0.2 at ζ = 2.5 reads β ≈ 2.4): the convention moves the amount, never the ratio k.

ε is the writer's drawing, not the reader's. Asked whether ε ≠ ½ moves the corner the way drawing V = cH moves a reader's: it does not. The reading k never sees ε, and the written speed is 0 at k = 1 for every ε. What ε moves is the writer's map: its two horizons, the one-way light speeds 1/(2ε) ahead and 1/(2(1 − ε)) behind, and its symmetry. Only ε = ½ writes the speed odd under the exchange k ↦ 1/k, the two legs drawn alike; in that sense Einstein's synchronisation is the writer's V = H. Placing a distant event is writing, so the choice sits with the writer.

Moving things. Where a thing goes and how fast is one point of the velocity hyperboloid, the hyperbolic plane (or space), drawn here as the Poincaré disc. A boost moves every point by one isometry: relative rapidities, the distances between points, are readings no frame changes, while the boundary at the rim is the sky of the first pane. The mass is carried along the address and never moves; energy and momentum, in the mass's unit, do. The pair (pc, mc²) has its corner at ζ = asinh 1 for every mass, so the corner belongs to the address, not the payload.

What is classical. Aberration as a Möbius map of the celestial sphere (Penrose 1959; Terrell 1959); the Doppler factor and Iν/ν³ (Rybicki and Lightman 1979, §4.9); Bondi's k-calculus (1964); the conventionality of simultaneity (Reichenbach 1928; Winnie 1970); velocity space as hyperbolic space (Varićak 1910; Fock); the Wigner–Thomas turn as the velocity triangle's area. The sorting is this project's; SR's formalism already writes the pieces apart.
