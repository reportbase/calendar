The Breadth Sweep

The breadth sweep

A breadth is an extent, continuous, like N but not an index, and each facing has one. One facing, one breadth: known, it is the quarter circle. Two facings, a breadth to the left and one to the right: known, the semicircle. Four facings, four breadths: known, the circle. Eight facings, eight breadths: known, the sphere. g is [0, 1], the same for every breadth; with the breadth known it is the radius from 1 to the breadth, r = 1 + g(B − 1). At g = 0 the radius is 1: the unit circle (with eight facings, the unit sphere). At g = 1 it is B: the breadth fully expressed. The turn from h toward v, with h relative to v and v relative to h (no division, no v/h), points to the place within the radius: x = r · turn / (π/2). The breadth here is known, divided evenly across the address space and read with no situated observer: the view from nowhere, with no home, no horizon and no corner. The breadths of h and v are known and equal, so both are normalized to 1: that is what makes it the unit circle. (A situated reader has the same geometry with the breadth unknown: the fisheye.)
