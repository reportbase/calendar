The fisheye sweep

Situation 3. h's breadth is known; v's breadth is not, so v is had only as a count of h's. The reader sweeps from home, step by step. The corner, where v reaches one h, is known even though v's breadth is not, so the sweep from home to the corner has two known ends and is taken in plain proportion: that span, the front side, is the working horizon, the one a situated reader experiences. Past the corner is the back side. It has no end to sweep to, so it is counted by doublings, 2 h, 4 h, 8 h, toward the horizon, which no count reaches. Nothing here uses an angle: the point v h's up the line through home is where the reader is looking.
